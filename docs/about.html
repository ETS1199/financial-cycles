What this measures

Reading a financial cycle before the crisis

This is the Advanced-Market overheating index from Chen & Svirydzenka's IMF study of financial-cycle early-warning indicators. The goal of this papers is to see if an economy's real equity prices and its output are far enough above their long-run trends to resemble the run-ups that preceded past banking crises.

The paper uses two metrics for 34 advanced economies: equity prices and output. These were the paper's strongest indicators in the loss function that minimizes missed crises and false alarms, outperforming credit-to-GDP and property prices. The index watches those two indicators and compares them against thresholds calibrated on historical crises, using the Christiano–Fitzgerald filter described in Methodology.

The two signals

Each is a percent deviation of the level from its calculated trend. A signal is triggered when the gap exceeds its threshold.

The index

Each signal is an on/off flag whether it is above its threshold or not. The index is their weighted sum, so it can only takefour values.

The weights (0.497 and 0.503) come from each indicator's crisis-prediction reliability in the paper. They are near-equal by coincidence, so the value is essentially a count of the signals above their thresholds.

Every gap comes from the Christiano–Fitzgerald band-pass filter. The full pipeline, from raw data through Harding–Pagan cycle dating to the filter mathematics is laid out under Methodology.

Method: Chen & Svirydzenka (2021), IMF WP/21/116 — Advanced-Market track.
